# Steward

Tasks and notes in one installable web app. Works offline; syncs through the owner's own Google Drive.

- Open https://csmit595.github.io/steward/ in Chrome and choose "Install".
- No build step: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules.
- No data is stored in this repository. Everything a person enters stays on their device and in their own Google Drive ("Steward" folder).

- 4.8: **Stats and the weekly review.** `#/stats` (sidebar → Goals & review; G then A): a week view (Monday to Sunday, Eastern) with back/forward arrows, and a quarter view. Every number comes from js/stats-core.js (pure, tested): **time by identity** against each weekly target, God and family first; **the quiet priorities** (days with time with God, timed family time Monday to Saturday toward its target with the hours still open, family evenings and Saturdays kept free of work, Sunday rest kept, seminary time, health); **customer promises** kept on time, finished late, and past due and still open; **goals** on track or behind the calendar; **fit**, the days the planned work fit in his hours (`computePlan` from js/dayplan.js, looked at afterwards); and tasks finished by area. Simple labelled bars, readable on the phone, no pie charts, no points, no streaks, no failure badges; Sunday never counts against anything (Prov. 27:23). `#/review` (G then R): a guided review of about 15 minutes, one step per screen with progress dots and Back/Next: look back in plain words; thanks and wins; what slipped (reschedule, drop, or "tell the customer early", which adds a `promise` task); the quiet priorities (yes / partly / no and a note); goals (on track / behind / change / drop, and the next task); next week (top three, each day's fit line, overloaded days flagged); optional **Reflect with Claude** (Sonnet through js/ai.js, reasoning from Proverbs and Matthew 5–7; skipped gracefully with no signal or no key; changes nothing); and a close that says "next week's necessary work fits" or what to move (Matt. 6:34). In the last ten days of a quarter it adds a quarter step (finish, carry over or drop each goal; set next quarter's). Each review is saved as a `reviews` item (`weekOf, answers, top3, statsSnapshot, noteId`) and as a note in the notebook "Weekly reviews". A repeating task "Weekly review" (id `weekly-review`) is made once; Settings → Weekly review picks the day and time (default Friday 4:00 PM, shared setting `reviewAt`), refused on Sunday, on Saturday family time and on any fixed block. `steward-summary.md` now carries "This week so far" and the last review's top three.
- 4.7: **Focus sessions and the family-time timer.** One tap from Today, a task's ⋯ menu, task details, a goal, an identity card, the sidebar (Focus, G then F) or by holding the plus button. Start sheet: what (task / goal / identity), length (25, 50, 90, custom), Standard or 5-50-5. **Fixed-block guard:** before starting it checks today's fixed blocks and calendar events, says what the session would run into and offers to shorten it to end in time; on the rest day it asks once. Running: a calm full-screen timer in the identity's color, Pause / End early, "I got pulled away" (counts interruptions; a web app cannot block other apps), a "turn on Do Not Disturb" line, the screen kept awake where supported, and a pill on other screens. Time is always worked out from saved moments (`startedAt`, `pauses`, `endedAt`; js/session-core.js, pure, tested), never a running counter, so it survives the screen locking, Steward closing, or switching devices; a session that ran out unseen is closed at the moment it ran out. At the end: a chime, then a check-in (focus 1–5, one line, "Mark task done", "Another round"). **Family time** is the same engine counting up: Start / Stop, no rating or interruptions; a fixed block marked Family offers "start the timer?" when it begins; a timer still running 5 hours on or past midnight asks once whether to keep it or stop it at a chosen time; "Add time" enters time by hand. Sunday's family minutes are kept but never counted toward the weekly target. Sessions live in `steward-goals.json` (`sessions`), with `endsAt` for the helper. **Helper:** each minute it also sends "Focus session done" at a running session's `endsAt` and "Family time: start the timer?" at the start of a Family block (unless the timer is running), by Web Push, the same way as reminders; tapping opens `#/focus` or starts the timer. Claude: `{"op":"upsert","type":"session","item":{"identity":"Family","minutes":90,"date":"2026-11-17","note":"…"}}` logs time by hand.
- 4.6: **Identities and goals.** Six identities, seeded in his order (Christ Worship, Family, Health, Work: CS Outdoors, Seminary & Church, Friends; editable, reorderable, deletable), each with optional weekly target, areas, tags and projects. 90-day goals sit under an identity: a checklist of tasks, a number to reach, or hours of focused time, with a calm "on track / behind the calendar" pace and "set aside" as an ordinary choice (Prov. 16:9). A task finds its identity with no extra tagging: its goal, then its project, then its tags, then its organizer area (`identityOf` in js/goals-core.js, pure, tested). Tasks get an optional `goalId`: a **Goal** picker in task details, `goal:<name>` in quick add (dictation: "goal <name>"), and a task's ⋯ menu makes it the goal's next task. Screens `#/goals` and `#/goal/<id>` (sidebar → Goals & review; G then O), and a folding "This quarter" strip on Today. A gentle note past 4 active goals; nothing is blocked. Fixed blocks can name the identity their time belongs to. New bundle `goals: ['identities','goals','sessions','reviews']` → `Steward/steward-goals.json`, merged field by field like tasks. Repeating tasks now keep a `doneLog` of when each round was finished. Claude: `{"op":"upsert","type":"identity","item":{...}}`, `{"op":"upsert","type":"goal","item":{"title":"…","identity":"Family","kind":"count","target":5}}`, `"goal":"<title>"` on a task, and `delete` for both (applied by the app; the helper leaves those files for it). `steward-summary.md` lists identities and active goals.
- 4.5: **Writing removed, notes protected.** The Writing section (Documents, Sources, citations, Word import/export, print layout, Ask Claude in Writing) is gone; papers and sermons live in Google Docs. Nothing is dropped: on the first launch each old document with real text becomes a note in the notebook "From Writing" and saved sources become one note (js/legacy-writing.js, js/migrate.js; the same ids on every device, so the phone and the Mac make the same notes), and only then are the old `docs.*`/`sources.*` files moved to the Drive trash. A Claude change naming a document or source gets a report saying Writing was removed. **Note version history** (js/versions-core.js, js/versions.js): a copy of a note's title and text is kept before an editing session's first change, when it ends (leaving the note, or 2 minutes idle), before a save that cuts the text to less than half, before a note goes to the trash, before a Claude change, and before a sync replaces local text with a very different version. The newest 20 are kept, plus the last of each day for 30 days, in `library/versions.<noteId>.json` (new itemwise type `versions`; copies from both devices are combined, never overwritten). Note menu → **Version history**: date, device, word count, read-only preview, "Show what is different", and Restore (which saves the current text first). **Safer trash**: "Empty trash" and "Delete forever" move notes to **Recently deleted** (link at the bottom of Trash) for 30 days with their attachments, then they are purged. Tests: `node --test steward/tests/*.test.mjs`.

- 4.3: organizes new tasks and notes. As a task or note is written, a "Suggested" row offers an area (CS Outdoors, Seminary, Church, Home/Family, Personal), finer tags (Embrace English, Sunday School, SCH 601, house project…), "customer promise" (the `promise` label), and a project or notebook; nothing is filed until **Confirm** is pressed (Add task, task details, the note editor). js/organize-core.js does the thinking with no browser APIs: public rules, a naive Bayes model trained on his confirmations ("lessons", including suggestions he took off), and the closest past lessons by meaning from the on-device MiniLM model (js/semantic.js). Lessons and "names and words it knows" (`orgwords`, personal names kept out of this public code) sync as Steward/steward-organize.json, and every device retrains from the same file, so the phone and the Mac suggest the same things. Settings → Organize automatically. Claude can add a known word with `{"op":"upsert","type":"orgword","item":{"word":"…","area":"home-family","tags":["…"]}}` (the app applies it; the helper leaves it for the app).
- 4.2: dictation works with no signal. Steward carries its own speech model (OpenAI's open Whisper, base English, about 77 MB, in vendor/models/Xenova/whisper-base.en) and runs it on the device in a background worker (js/whisper-worker.js); js/listen-device.js listens through the microphone, cuts speech into phrases at natural pauses and sends each to the model. Settings → Speak instead of type: Automatic (Chrome's speech service with signal, the device model without, switching over mid-dictation if the signal drops) or Always on this device (private). The model downloads by itself the first time a device is on Wi-Fi (a Mac any time), or with the button there or Download everything.
